WaterProject Threat Console

Catches attacks on a Siemens-controlled water process by checking whether the controller's valve and pump commands match what it normally does. Every number here was computed on our IBM LinuxONE server.

How the controller check works

Attackers in the 2026 water-utility campaign reached controllers and changed what they told pumps and valves to do. A changed command can still produce readings that look physically normal, so we check the command itself.

1. Read the plantWater level, its high and low setpoints, flow, pressure and the hydro-turbine demand, every second.
2. Expect a commandA model trained only on normal operation predicts the valve and pump command the Siemens S7-300 would normally issue.
3. CompareThe gap between the actual and expected command, in multiples of its normal size.
4. AlarmWhen the gap stays above the alarm level for 10 seconds, raise an alarm and write a plain-English brief.
